# Eval record: the 0.3.1 wording edit (2026-11-21)

Status: a rewrite of the 0.3.0 body in shorter sentences, meant to change no rule. Two blind
reviews read drafts, an independent review compared the text that was run with the 0.3.0 body
paragraph by paragraph, and 22 smoke runs were made on Claude Code. The shipped text (arm F) is
the text of 16 of those runs (arm E) with four small fixes from that review, and has 5 runs of
its own. Codex was not run to the end: the account's usage limit stopped the three runs
planned for it. Per-run results: [2026-11-21-0.3.1-runs.csv](2026-11-21-0.3.1-runs.csv).
Tasks, graders, engine and harness are those of [the 0.3.0 record](2026-11-21-0.3.0.md).

## Question

The owner found the body's sentences too long and asked for shorter ones that keep the
context; he allowed sentences to be written anew. Does the rewritten body still say what the
0.3.0 body (arm D) says, and does the model still do what the 0.3.0 record observed?

## The edit

Nine paragraphs are rewritten. They are listed with their full old and new text under
`body_changes` in [provenance](../provenance.json), as five edits, because paragraphs that
follow each other are given together.

- Long sentences are split inside their paragraphs, and some are reworded where a split alone
  left them awkward. Each paragraph keeps its conditions, actions, limits and reasons:
  [dia-regula 3.0.0](https://github.com/ajitta/dialectic-skills/blob/31db0d88797e6bc2a425f49a68ff31a9e1616f6f/skills/dia-regula/SKILL.md)
  counts the paragraph, with its sub-items, as the unit that must hold a rule whole, and does
  not ask for one sentence.
- Section 1's paragraph on assumptions becomes a lead sentence with a labelled sub-item per
  case, worked through in order, with the ask-before-implementing case nested under the case it
  is an exception to. The 0.3.0 record listed the chained sentence as a limit.
- Wording that an earlier record had tied to behaviour is kept as it was: the two labels and
  their values, "a pick you state is still your pick", "even for a one-line fix", "not
  executed; inspection only", and the threshold "more than a single reply or a single obvious
  edit", which is now stated twice in its paragraph.

## Reviews

- Two blind reviews against dia-regula 3.0.0, each by a separate agent given the old text, a
  draft and the rules file, and asked whether any edit changes what a model does: one of a
  split-only draft, one of the rewritten draft. Neither draft was run. Their findings
  overlapped, and those below were fixed before the first run:
  - Section 1: as a sentence of its own, the duty to read the record behind a claim had lost
    the threshold and its reason, and "such a request" in the next sentence could be read as "a
    request that rests on a summary". Now: "If that request rests on a summary or a claim …,
    also read the record behind it before building on it, for the same reason", and the
    sentence on the reply restates the threshold.
  - Section 1, assumptions: the lead sentence had stretched "you would otherwise only mention"
    over every case, the order (files first) was only implied, and the case that asks before
    implementing stood beside the case it is an exception to. The lead now says "work through
    these cases in order", the phrase is back on the file case, and the exception is nested.
  - Section 3: the reason had moved off the three instructions it supported; it is back.
  - Section 4: "unless the user asked for that run" no longer covered "treat it as a check
    that cannot run"; both sentences now carry the condition.
- The text with those fixes (E) was checked by the editor and then run. An independent review
  of the branch afterwards (a separate agent, read-only) compared the 0.3.0 body with E
  paragraph by paragraph and re-graded the runs from their workspaces. It found no condition,
  limit, fallback or reason lost except in three places, and no count or quote mismatch. The
  three places and one rendering point are fixed in F:
  1. the last case of the assumptions list had no order of its own; it now reads "Something is
     still unclear after you check the files";
  2. the nested item was indented two spaces, which some Markdown renderers flatten; it is
     indented four;
  3. section 3: "mention unrelated dead code if you notice it" had been cut off from its
     reason; it is back inside the sentence that carries it;
  4. section 4: "do not run as a check code whose run …" was hard to parse; it reads "Do not
     run code whose run you could not reverse (…) as a check unless the user asked for that
     run."
- F itself was checked by the editor only. The review reports are kept by the editor and are
  not published.

### The two labelled lines

One recorded item disagreed with the 0.3.0 record and was followed up. `P2-E-1` reached the
outcome but its reply did not open with the two labelled lines, where the 0.3.0 wordings had
printed them on every P run on Claude Code (C and C2 10 of 10, D 8 of 8). The edit had
rewritten those sentences, so three more runs were made under a rule written first: two of E
and one of D on P2. All three printed the lines. E therefore opened with them in 5 of 6 P
runs, and F, which leaves those sentences as E has them, in 2 of 2. The lines were absent, as
expected, on R3 (a single obvious edit) and on F1 (a reply form the user asked for), and
present on O1, R2, S1 and every R4 run; N1, R1 and V1 had none.

One miss in eight may be chance or a small cost of the rewrite; these counts cannot tell.

## Reading against the rules written before the runs

- The rule for E: kept if the review finds no meaning change, or its findings are fixed before
  the runs, and every cell meets the expectation the 0.3.0 record wrote for it. The draft
  reviews' findings were fixed before the runs and every cell met its expectation. The
  follow-up rule for P2 (E stays if both further E runs print the lines) held.
- The independent review then found three places where E still differed from the 0.3.0 body.
  The rule written before the F runs: F ships if its five runs meet their expectations. They
  did.
- This does not show that the rewrite leaves behaviour unchanged. It shows that no expectation
  was missed at one run per cell.

## Deviations from the plan

- The plan was written for a split-only edit. Before any run the owner allowed sentences to be
  written anew, and the text was rewritten; it was changed again after the draft reviews, and
  again (E to F) after the independent review. Notes in the plan record each step before the
  runs it governs.
- The three Codex runs (P1s, P2, P3) did not finish. Two started, read the 0.3.1 text and ran
  5 and 6 commands before the account's usage limit stopped them, without an edit or a reply;
  the third stopped before any turn. Their three slots went to the P2 follow-up.
- 22 runs in all: E 16, D 1, F 5. The plan had 17.
- Neither E nor F had a blind review of its own before it was run; E had the independent
  review afterwards.

## Limits

- One model, one day, one run on most cells. The edit is classified as wording only, which
  this repository checks by one independent review; the runs are a smoke check on top of that
  and do not measure anything.
- The shipped text has runs of its own on five cells; the rest were run on E, which differs
  from it in four places.
- Codex was not run on the 0.3.1 text.
- The labelled lines were missing in one of eight P runs of the 0.3.1 wordings, against none
  of eighteen on Claude Code before.
- The limits of the 0.3.0 record carry over: the tasks were written by the editor, the runs
  were headless, and the two sentences added last in 0.3.0 remain unshown.
