# Eval record: the 0.3.1 wording edit (2026-11-21)

Status: a wording edit of the 0.3.0 body, checked by two blind reviews and 17 smoke runs on
Claude Code. Codex was not run: the account's usage limit stopped the three runs planned for
it. Per-run results: [2026-11-21-0.3.1-runs.csv](2026-11-21-0.3.1-runs.csv). Tasks, graders,
engine and harness are those of [the 0.3.0 record](2026-11-21-0.3.0.md).

## Question

The owner found the body's sentences too long and asked for shorter ones that keep the
context; he allowed sentences to be written anew. Does the rewritten body (arm E) still say
what the 0.3.0 body (arm D) says, and does the model still do what the 0.3.0 record observed?

## The edit

Nine paragraphs are rewritten. They are listed with their full old and new text under
`body_changes` in [provenance](../provenance.json), as five edits, because paragraphs that
follow each other are given together.

- Long sentences are split inside their paragraphs, and some are reworded where a split alone
  left them awkward. Each paragraph keeps its conditions, actions, limits and reasons:
  dia-regula 3.0.0 counts the paragraph, with its sub-items, as the unit that must hold a rule
  whole, and does not ask for one sentence.
- Section 1's paragraph on assumptions becomes a lead sentence with a labelled sub-item per
  case, worked through in order, with the ask-before-implementing case nested under the case it
  is an exception to. The 0.3.0 record listed the chained sentence as a limit.
- Wording that an earlier record had tied to behaviour is kept as it was: the two labels and
  their values, "a pick you state is still your pick", "even for a one-line fix", "not
  executed; inspection only", and the threshold "more than a single reply or a single obvious
  edit", which is now stated twice in its paragraph.

## Reviews

Two blind reviews against dia-regula 3.0.0, each by a separate agent given the old text, the
new text and the rules file, and asked whether any edit changes what a model does: one of a
split-only draft, one of the rewritten draft. Neither draft was run. Both reviews found the
same meaning changes, and all were fixed before the first run:

- Section 1: as a sentence of its own, the duty to read the record behind a claim had lost the
  threshold and its reason, and "such a request" in the next sentence could be read as "a
  request that rests on a summary". Now: "If that request rests on a summary or a claim …, also
  read the record behind it before building on it, for the same reason", and the sentence on
  the reply restates the threshold.
- Section 1, assumptions: the lead sentence had stretched "you would otherwise only mention"
  over every case, the order (files first) was only implied, and the case that asks before
  implementing stood beside the case it is an exception to. The lead now says "work through
  these cases in order", the phrase is back on the file case, and the exception is nested.
- Section 3: the reason had moved off the three instructions it supported; it is back.
- Section 4: "unless the user asked for that run" no longer covered "treat it as a check that
  cannot run"; both sentences now carry the condition.

The fixes follow the reviewers' rewordings. The fixed text was checked against dia-regula 3.0.0
by the editor and had no third blind review.

### The two labelled lines

One recorded item disagreed with the 0.3.0 record and was followed up. `P2-E-1` reached the
outcome but its reply did not open with the two labelled lines, where the 0.3.0 wordings
printed them on every P run (C and C2 10 of 10, D 8 of 8). The edit had rewritten those
sentences, so three more runs were made under a rule written first: two of E and one of D on
P2. All three printed the lines. E therefore opened with them in 5 of 6 P runs. They were
absent, as expected, on R3 (a single obvious edit) and on F1 (a reply form the user asked
for), and present on O1, R2, S1 and both R4 runs; N1, R1 and V1 had none.

One miss in six may be chance or a small cost of the rewrite; these counts cannot tell.

## Reading against the rules written before the runs

The rule: E is kept if the review finds no meaning change, or its findings are fixed before
the runs, and every cell meets the expectation the 0.3.0 record wrote for it. Both hold. The
follow-up rule for P2 (E stays if both further E runs print the lines) also holds.

## Deviations from the plan

- The plan was written for a split-only edit. Before any run the owner allowed sentences to be
  written anew, and the text was rewritten; then it was changed once more after the reviews.
  Two notes in the plan record this, each written before the first run.
- The three Codex runs (P1s, P2, P3) did not happen. Their three slots went to the P2
  follow-up, so the total stayed 17: E 16, D 1.
- The fixed text had no blind review of its own.

## Limits

- One model, one day, one run on most cells. The edit is classified as wording only, which
  this repository checks by one independent review; the runs are a smoke check on top of that
  and do not measure anything.
- Codex was not run on the 0.3.1 text.
- The labelled lines were missing in one of six P runs, against none of eighteen before.
- The limits of the 0.3.0 record carry over: the tasks were written by the editor, the runs
  were headless, and the two sentences added last in 0.3.0 remain unshown.
